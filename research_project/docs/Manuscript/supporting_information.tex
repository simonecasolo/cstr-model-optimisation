\section*{S1.\; Stochastic Data Generation and Scenario Overview}
\label{sec:si_data_generation}
% =============================================================================

\paragraph{Simulation pipeline.}
Observation windows are generated by integrating the degraded closed-loop model
(Eqs.~(3)--(6) of the main text) with the Euler--Maruyama scheme at a fixed step
of $\Delta t = 0.1$~min.  Two independent noise layers are applied.  \emph{Process
noise} is injected at each integration step as an additive Gaussian increment:
$\boldsymbol{\sigma} = [\sigma_C,\,\sigma_T,\,\sigma_{T_c}] =
[5\times10^{-4}$~mol~L$^{-1}$\,min$^{-1/2}$,\;
$0.1$~K~min$^{-1/2}$,\;$0.1$~K~min$^{-1/2}]$,
consistent with mild process variability at steady state.  \emph{Sensor noise}
is applied post-simulation as a zero-mean Gaussian draw with standard deviation
equal to 0.5\% of each channel's running maximum, representing a typical
industrial-grade sensor specification.

Each replicate is initialised from the nominal closed-loop steady state, computed
by integrating the deterministic ODE to convergence with a high-accuracy adaptive
solver.  Initialising from the steady state removes startup transients and ensures
that the 60-minute window is representative of stationary operation around the
prescribed $(\alpha^*,\beta^*)$ operating point.  The four observable channels
$[C(t),\,T(t),\,T_c(t),\,Q_c(t)]$ are subsampled to a grid of $\Delta t_\mathrm{out}
= 0.5$~min, yielding one $120\times4$ observation matrix per replicate.

\paragraph{Dataset structure.}
The full System~I dataset comprises $8$ scenarios $\times$ $50$ independent
stochastic replicates $= 400$ observation windows.  The eight scenarios
(Table~1 of the main text) span open- and closed-loop configurations and a
range of fault severities; their ground-truth parameters $(\alpha^*,\beta^*)$
are summarised in Table~1 of the main text.  Each replicate
within a scenario shares the same ground-truth $(\alpha^*,\beta^*)$ but was
integrated with an independent random seed, so the 50 replicates per scenario
are exchangeable draws from $p(\mathbf{x}\mid\alpha^*,\beta^*)$ at that
operating point.

\paragraph{Scenario trajectories.}
Figure~\ref{fig:si_scenarios} shows six representative replicates per closed-loop
scenario across all four observable channels.  The bold black trace is the
per-scenario replicate mean.

Several features are immediately apparent.  First, the reactor temperature $T$
is tightly regulated near the setpoint $T_\mathrm{sp} = 312.5$~K in all
non-saturating scenarios: the PI controller maintains the same temperature
profile regardless of whether the jacket is fouled (Sc\,2) or the catalyst
is decaying (Sc\,3), confirming the closed-loop masking mechanism discussed in
Section~2.2 of the main text.  Second, the coolant flow $Q_c$ shows
qualitatively distinct behaviour across scenarios: jacket fouling (Sc\,2) drives
$Q_c$ to $\approx 210$~L~min$^{-1}$, while catalyst decay (Sc\,3) lowers it
to $\approx 50$~L~min$^{-1}$, and severe fouling (Sc\,5) saturates the valve
at $Q_{c,\max} = 400$~L~min$^{-1}$.  Third, the concentration channel $C$
shifts distinctly between scenarios with different $\alpha^*$: catalyst decay
(Sc\,3, $\alpha^*=0.70$) raises $C_\mathrm{ss}$ from $\approx 0.018$ to
$\approx 0.026$~mol~L$^{-1}$, while jacket fouling (Sc\,2) produces a modest
downward shift attributable to the residual thermal coupling through $T_c$.
The sensor-drift scenario (Sc\,7, $\delta_T = +2$~K) is indistinguishable from
Sc\,1 in the $C$ and $T_c$ channels but shows a systematic upward offset in
the $T$ channel, motivating the four-dimensional
$(\alpha,\beta,\delta_T,\delta_{C_i})$ extension explored in future work.

\section*{S2.\; Prior Predictive Check}
\label{sec:si_prior_predictive}
% =============================================================================

A necessary condition for valid amortised inference is that the prior-predictive
distribution --- the distribution of summary statistics produced by prior draws ---
covers the evaluation observations.  To verify this, we drew 500 parameter vectors
$\boldsymbol{\theta}=(\alpha,\beta)$ from the uniform prior~$\mathcal{U}([0.4,1.2]^2)$
and simulated each through the stochastic closed-loop model to obtain synthetic
60-minute observation windows.  For every feature in the 29-D summary vector, the
$[2.5\%,\,97.5\%]$ interval of the prior-predictive distribution was compared against
the observed values from all six closed-loop evaluation scenarios (Sc\,1--5, Sc\,7).
At least 80\% of the observed values per feature fall inside this interval for the large
majority of the 29-D summary vector, confirming that the simulator faithfully spans the
evaluation regime. Under the matched-protocol warm-start pipeline used for the
production posterior (Section~6.1 of the main text), three temperature-related features
($T_\mathrm{mean}$, $T_\mathrm{final\_mean}$, $\int|T-T_\mathrm{sp}|\,\mathrm{d}t$) fall
below the 80\% threshold when pooled across all six closed-loop scenarios (72.7\%,
76.0\%, and 70.7\% respectively at $N_\mathrm{prior}=500$; stable to within Monte Carlo
noise up to $N_\mathrm{prior}=2{,}000$, confirming this is not a sample-size effect ---
see below).

\textit{Root cause, identified by decomposing the pooled coverage per scenario.} The
shortfall is not spread across the six evaluation scenarios: it is almost entirely
attributable to Sc\,7, the sensor-drift scenario. Sc\,7's temperature-related feature
values lie \emph{entirely outside} the prior-predictive envelope (0\%, 12\%, and 2\%
per-scenario coverage for the three features respectively) because its $+2$~K
temperature offset is an additive sensor bias applied after simulation
($\delta_T$, Section~S1), not a function of the closed-loop dynamics that the
2-D $(\alpha,\beta)$ prior parameterises --- no draw from $\mathcal{U}([0.4,1.2]^2)$
can reproduce it, regardless of how many simulations are drawn. Excluding Sc\,7,
pooled coverage across the remaining five scenarios rises to 87.2\%, 88.8\%, and
84.4\% respectively, comfortably above threshold; the residual imperfection there
comes from Sc\,5 (severe fouling, actuator saturation), whose coverage is 40--54\%
per feature and which --- unlike Sc\,7 --- \emph{is} a function of $(\alpha,\beta)$
and would respond to a wider prior, since a broader box increases the fraction of
prior draws that saturate the actuator.

We also directly tested whether more prior simulations close this gap: repeating the
check at $N_\mathrm{prior}\in\{500,1{,}000,1{,}500,2{,}000\}$ from one shared pool of
2,000 draws changes the $[2.5\%,97.5\%]$ interval width by \emph{less than 3\%} and
the resulting coverage by less than 1 percentage point at every budget tested,
confirming $N_\mathrm{prior}=500$ was already fully converged for this diagnostic ---
the shortfall is a scope mismatch (Sc\,7's $\delta_T$ dimension is absent from the
prior being checked), not a Monte Carlo sampling-noise artefact, and is not expected
to affect any reported posterior-accuracy or classification result, all of which
condition on the observed data directly rather than on this prior-predictive coverage
statistic.

Figure~\ref{fig:si_simulator_sanity} illustrates the check for four representative
features, including the two physics-informed proxies that carry most of the
identifying information about $(\alpha,\beta)$ (see Section~3.1 of the main text).
The observed scenario means (vertical markers) are well contained within the
prior-predictive histograms for every feature shown, with no scenario lying
systematically outside the prior envelope.

\section*{S3.\; Training Budget Sensitivity}
\label{sec:si_sensitivity}
% =============================================================================

To confirm that the choice of $n_\mathrm{sim}=10{,}000$ training simulations is
sufficient, we trained three posteriors using MAF density estimators, under the same
matched scenario-specific warm-start protocol as the production posterior (Section~6.1
of the main text), at budgets of $1{,}000$, $5{,}000$, and $10{,}000$ prior draws, and
evaluated each on the healthy scenario (Sc\,1, $\alpha^*=\beta^*=1.0$) and the
jacket-fouling scenario (Sc\,2, $\beta^*=0.70$).

For Sc\,2, the posterior mean of $\beta$ is $\hat{\beta}=0.747$ at
$n_\mathrm{sim}=1{,}000$, $0.735$ at $n_\mathrm{sim}=5{,}000$, and $0.733$ at
$n_\mathrm{sim}=10{,}000$ --- essentially flat beyond $n_\mathrm{sim}=5{,}000$, which is
the basis for choosing $n_\mathrm{sim}=10{,}000$ for the production posterior. 90\%
credible-interval coverage of the true parameters on Sc\,1 is achieved at all three
budgets for $\alpha$, and at $n_\mathrm{sim}\geq5{,}000$ for $\beta$ ($n_\mathrm{sim}=
1{,}000$ misses); on Sc\,2, $\beta$'s 90\% interval contains the true value at
$n_\mathrm{sim}=5{,}000$ but not at $1{,}000$ or $10{,}000$ (a single-replicate
diagnostic, not a calibration claim --- see Section~S6 for the actual SBC-based
calibration assessment across many replicates).

\textit{Discrepancy with the production posterior, and why it is expected.} This MAF
ablation's $\hat\beta\approx0.73$--$0.75$ on Sc\,2 \emph{overshoots} the true value of
$0.70$, whereas the production NSF posterior (Section~6.1, Table VI
of the main text) gives $\hat\beta=0.6978$, i.e. essentially unbiased. Both use the
matched warm-start protocol, so the discrepancy is not a training/evaluation mismatch;
it reflects (i) a different density-estimator family (MAF here vs. the production NSF)
and (ii) a different random seed. This is expected variation across the design choices
this ablation exists to explore, and does not indicate a problem with the production
posterior used everywhere else in the manuscript --- Figure~\ref{fig:si_sensitivity}
below should be read as a budget-sensitivity comparison among these three MAF runs, not
as an alternative estimate of the production posterior's accuracy.

\section{summary statistics}

\paragraph{Group~1 --- Per-channel base statistics (20 features).}
For each of the four observable channels $[C, T, T_c, Q_c]$, five
scalar statistics are computed over the full 60-minute window: the
arithmetic mean, standard deviation, ordinary-least-squares slope,
minimum, and maximum.  These 20 features capture the level, variability,
trend, and range of each signal and form the baseline subset against
which the additional groups are evaluated.

\paragraph{Group~2 --- Final-window means (4 features).}
The arithmetic mean of each channel over the final 25\% of the observation
window (minutes 45--60) provides an estimate of the current
quasi-steady-state operating point, less contaminated by fault-onset
transients than the whole-window mean.  Under stationary closed-loop
operation these four values converge to the steady-state measurements
$C_\mathrm{ss}$, $T_\mathrm{ss}$, $T_{c,\mathrm{ss}}$, and
$Q_{c,\mathrm{ss}}$.

\paragraph{Group~3 --- Control aggregates (3 features).}
Three aggregates quantify the operational burden placed on the PI
controller during the observation window:
%
\begin{itemize}
  \item $\int_0^{60}\lvert T(t)-T_\mathrm{sp}\rvert\,\mathrm{d}t$: the
    time-integrated absolute temperature error, which increases whenever
    the controller is unable to maintain the setpoint, most prominently
    during the actuator-saturation regime of Sc\,5.
  \item $f_\mathrm{sat,\,low}$: the fraction of time steps at which
    $Q_c = Q_{c,\min} = 0$~L~min$^{-1}$, indicating fully-closed valve
    saturation.
  \item $f_\mathrm{sat,\,high}$: the fraction of time steps at which
    $Q_c = Q_{c,\max} = 400$~L~min$^{-1}$, indicating fully-open
    saturation.
\end{itemize}
%
All three aggregates equal zero under healthy, non-saturating closed-loop
operation and become the dominant diagnostic features in the degraded
regimes where the per-channel statistics carry reduced discriminating power.

\paragraph{Group~4 --- Physics-informed proxies (2 features).}
Two features are derived by algebraic inversion of the closed-loop
steady-state balance equations, making them direct proxies for the
identifiable parameter products $\beta\!\cdot\!U\!A$ and
$\alpha\!\cdot\!k_0$:
%
\begin{align}
  s_{U\!A} &= \frac{\bar{T}-\bar{T}_c}{\bar{Q}_c},
  \label{eq:ua_proxy}\\[4pt]
  s_{k_0} &= \ln\!\left(\frac{\bar{C}}{C_i - \bar{C}}\right),
  \label{eq:k0_proxy}
\end{align}
%
where $\bar{(\cdot)}$ denotes the whole-window mean of the respective
channel.  Under non-saturating PI control at steady state,
$s_{U\!A}\propto(\beta\!\cdot\!U\!A)^{-1}$ follows from the jacket energy
balance, and $s_{k_0}\propto(\alpha\!\cdot\!k_0)^{-1}$ follows from the
component balance at fixed conversion.  Used alone, these two features
achieve 99.3\% cross-validated Linear Discriminant Analysis (LDA) \citep{Zhou2021}
accuracy on the eight-scenario dataset, confirming that they encode nearly all of the identifiable
information about $(\alpha,\beta)$ accessible from stationary, noise-free
steady-state observations.  Figure~\ref{fig:physics_scatter} illustrates
this separation directly: all eight scenarios occupy well-separated,
compact clusters in the $(s_{U\!A},\,s_{k_0})$ plane.

\section*{S3a.\; Extended Hyperparameter Ablation Study}
\label{sec:si_ablation}
% =============================================================================

A reviewer of a closely related paper by the same group (Casolo et al.,
\emph{Engineering Applications of Artificial Intelligence}, in press) requested that
training-set size, density-estimator depth, and optimiser/architecture choices be
varied and justified rather than fixed without comment. This section reports that
ablation for System~I, across three axes: training budget ($n_\mathrm{sim}\in\{500,
1{,}000, 2{,}500, 5{,}000, 10{,}000\}$), Neural Spline Flow (NSF) architecture width
and depth (hidden features $\times$ transforms $\in\{(64,3),(128,5)\,[\mathrm{production}],
(256,5),(128,8)\}$), and density-estimator family (NSF vs.\ Masked Autoregressive
Flow, MAF, at the production width/depth). All nine configurations are trained under
the same matched scenario-specific warm-start protocol as the production posterior
(Section~6.1 of the main text), on prefixes of one shared 10,000-draw training bank
(so the budget axis tests genuinely nested subsets, not independent re-draws), and
evaluated against one shared, 300-draw held-out simulation-based-calibration (SBC)
set never used for training.

\textit{Point accuracy is stable across the entire grid; formal joint calibration
is not, and this is a genuine, reportable finding rather than a training bug.} The
Sc\,2 $\beta$ posterior mean stays within $0.690$--$0.728$ across all nine
configurations --- a $\pm5\%$ band around the true value of $0.70$ regardless of
budget, width, depth, or estimator family, confirming that $n_\mathrm{sim}=10{,}000$
with the production architecture is not an arbitrary choice: smaller budgets and
smaller networks are already adequate for point estimation, and larger ones buy
nothing more. Formal SBC calibration is a different story: \textbf{none of the nine
configurations in this table passes simulation-based calibration on both $\alpha$
and $\beta$ simultaneously at $p>0.05$} against the shared held-out set, including
the configuration matching the production architecture and budget. This does not
contradict the main text's reported SBC pass ($p=0.21/0.13$, Section~6.1): that
result is from the specific, separately-promoted production posterior
(\texttt{results/sbi\_posterior\_final.pkl}), trained on its own training-data
realisation, whereas every row in this table is an independent retrain on a
freshly drawn (though nested, for the budget axis) dataset with the network's own
random initialisation. The pattern --- one marginal passing comfortably while the
other fails, with which marginal fails varying unsystematically across
configurations, and C2ST scores staying close to the uninformative $0.5$ baseline
throughout (0.51--0.57, not shown) rather than indicating gross miscalibration ---
is the same qualitative signature already documented for this project's System~II
posteriors (SNPE training is seed- and training-realisation-sensitive for this
class of problem; a single training run is not sufficient evidence of calibration
either way). \textbf{The practical implication is that any single SNPE training
run, including the currently-promoted production posterior, should be understood as
one draw from a calibration-sensitive process, not a guaranteed-calibrated
artefact}; the production posterior's SBC pass is real but should be corroborated
by a multi-seed sweep --- train several seeds at the production architecture and
budget, and confirm calibration holds across the ensemble --- before being treated
as fully settled, mirroring the multi-seed-ensemble-and-select protocol already used
to promote a calibrated System~II posterior. This corroboration has not yet been run
for System~I and is recorded here as the concrete next step.

\section*{S5.\; Complete Summary-Statistic Definitions and Feature Ablation}
\label{sec:si_features}
% =============================================================================

Table~\ref{tab:si_summary_stats} lists all 29 features that form the summary-statistic
vector $\mathbf{s}\in\mathbb{R}^{29}$ used throughout this work.  The four groups
correspond to those described in Section~3.1 of the main text.

Table~\ref{tab:si_feature_ablation} reports five-fold stratified cross-validated
LDA classification accuracy for seven feature subsets on the 400-window
System~I dataset.  The physics-2 subset (Group~4, two features) alone achieves
99.3\% accuracy, confirming that the steady-state proxy features encode nearly
all scenario-separating information.  The remaining 27 features are necessary
not to improve classification accuracy --- which saturates at 100\% with as few
as four final-window means --- but to correctly quantify posterior uncertainty
under noise, transients, and actuator saturation.  This distinction between
classification accuracy and posterior calibration is central to the argument of
the main text: the 29-D vector is chosen to provide accurate full posterior
distributions, not merely correct fault labels.

Beyond the two-feature theoretical minimum, four practical effects require
additional features: sensor noise amplifies in the ratio $s_{U\!A}$ at moderate
$Q_c$ values, making it preferable to supply the three component channels
separately; slope and final-window features carry transient information during
fault onset; the saturation-fraction features take over from $s_{U\!A}$ when
the valve is fully open (Sc\,5); and the standard deviation of $Q_c$ distinguishes
open-loop windows (constant $Q_c$) from closed-loop ones.

\section*{S6.\; Posterior Calibration: Simulation-Based Calibration Protocol
  and Results}
\label{sec:si_sbc}
% =============================================================================

\paragraph{Protocol.}
Posterior calibration is assessed using simulation-based calibration~%
\citep{Talts2018}.  In SBC, $N_\mathrm{SBC}$ parameter vectors
$\boldsymbol{\theta}^{(i)}$ are drawn independently from the prior; for each draw
a synthetic observation $\mathbf{x}^{(i)}$ is simulated and the posterior is sampled
to obtain $L$ approximate draws $\{\tilde{\boldsymbol{\theta}}^{(i)}_\ell\}_{\ell=1}^L$.
The rank of the true parameter among the posterior draws,
%
\begin{equation}
  r^{(i)}_j
  = \sum_{\ell=1}^{L}
    \mathbf{1}\!\left\{\tilde{\theta}^{(i)}_{\ell,j} < \theta^{(i)}_j\right\},
\end{equation}
%
is recorded for each marginal $j\in\{\alpha,\beta\}$.  A well-calibrated posterior
yields rank statistics that are uniformly distributed on $\{0,1,\ldots,L\}$ across
all $N_\mathrm{SBC}$ test cases.  Uniformity is assessed via the
Kolmogorov--Smirnov (KS) test and the two-sample classifier score
(C2ST;~\citealt{LopezPaz2017}): a KS $p$-value above $0.05$ indicates that
uniformity cannot be rejected at the 5\% level; a C2ST score below $0.52$ indicates
that the rank distribution is indistinguishable from uniform by a trained binary
classifier~\citep{TejeroCantero2020}.  We use $N_\mathrm{SBC}=500$ test cases and
$L=1{,}000$ posterior draws per case.

\paragraph{Results.}
The KS $p$-values for the production NSF posterior, trained and evaluated under the
matched scenario-specific warm-start protocol (Section~6.1 of the main text), are
$0.30$ ($\alpha$) and $0.75$ ($\beta$) in the run shown in
Figure~\ref{fig:si_sbc_ranks} --- rank uniformity cannot be rejected at the 5\% level
for either marginal. The C2ST scores are $0.56$ ($\alpha$) and $0.52$ ($\beta$), both
near the uninformative baseline of 0.5.

\textit{On run-to-run variation.} The $N_\mathrm{SBC}=500$ test draws are not fully
seeded end-to-end (the \texttt{sbi} library's own posterior-sampling internals do not
consistently respect an upstream \texttt{torch} seed), so repeated from-scratch SBC
runs against the same production posterior give different exact $p$-values. Across
three independent runs performed for this document, KS $p$ ranged over
$0.30$--$0.98$ ($\alpha$) and $0.18$--$0.78$ ($\beta$), and C2ST ranged over
$0.51$--$0.56$ for both marginals --- every run rejects neither marginal's
uniformity, and none approaches the $p=0.05$ threshold. The substantive conclusion
(well-calibrated under the matched protocol) is therefore robust to this variation;
only the reported decimal values are run-specific. The main text reports $0.21$/$0.13$
from an earlier such run, consistent with this range.

\textit{Correction relative to an earlier version of this document.} An earlier,
pre-matched-protocol training/evaluation pipeline reported KS $p$-values of $0.016$
($\alpha$) and $0.014$ ($\beta$) for a superficially similar posterior, formally
rejecting rank uniformity, together with an interpretation that this rejection
reflected an irreducible structural information deficit. Diagnostic work reported in
the main text traced that specific miscalibration to a training/evaluation
initial-condition mismatch --- a fixable protocol artefact, not a property of the
plant or controller --- and the corrected, matched-protocol posterior reported here
passes SBC cleanly. This does not overturn the genuine, representation-independent
Fisher-information asymmetry between $\alpha$ and $\beta$ (Section~4.3 of the main
text, $I_{\alpha\alpha}/I_{\beta\beta}=250$--$500\times$, confirmed by the
embedding-net irreducibility experiment): that asymmetry is real and much smaller in
magnitude than the bias the earlier, uncorrected protocol produced, and is consistent
with a well-calibrated posterior that is nonetheless less informative about $\beta$
than about $\alpha$.

\paragraph{Interpretation.}
A well-calibrated posterior (KS $p>0.05$, C2ST $\approx 0.5$ for both marginals) is
the expected outcome once the training and evaluation protocols are matched: SBC
rank uniformity certifies that the amortised posterior faithfully represents its own
uncertainty given the information available in a closed-loop observation window, not
that both parameters are equally well identified. The $\beta$ marginal remains wider
than $\alpha$'s (Section~4.3, Section~S3), consistent with the PI controller's
suppression of the temperature channel's contribution to $I_{\beta\beta}$ --- a
genuine, representation-independent information deficit, distinct from the
now-corrected calibration artefact described above.
